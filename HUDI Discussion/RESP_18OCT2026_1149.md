## 2. Direct Zoho Inventory handoff

The reported behavior is aligned with the intended design:

- Payment is verified before order creation.
- A webhook can create the order even when browser return is blocked.
- The outbox worker creates and confirms the Zoho sales order.
- Existing sales orders are adopted instead of duplicated.
- Failed synchronization leaves the paid order intact and exposes a retry path in Admin.
- Provider refusal messages are translated into EffyKlean-controlled messages.

Four qualifications remain.

### A. Forced stock of 999

The sandbox has no stock, so the test harness forced stock to 999. This does not establish real stock availability, stock reservation or inventory decrement behavior. It can still provide valuable evidence of the payment-to-order and synchronization paths. Record the limitation rather than discarding the test.

### B. Confirmed but unpaid and uninvoiced sales orders

The agent says this is expected because invoicing is handled by Zoho-side automation. That may be correct, but is not yet verified. Distinguish:

- Payment confirmed by EffyKlean/payment provider.
- Sales order created and confirmed in Zoho.
- Invoice generated.
- Invoice and payment status synchronized appropriately.
- Shipment initiated at the permitted time.

A confirmed Zoho sales order marked unpaid is not, by itself, evidence of payment failure. Equally, do not assume invoice automation works merely because the sales order exists.

### C. Five old stub tasks marked done

Closing five pending tasks without a Zoho call is safe only if their task semantics genuinely mean that no provider synchronization was required. If “done” means the intended Zoho synchronization succeeded, operational records could be misleading. Clarify the status meaning and treatment of those five specific tasks; do not request a broad outbox review.

### D. Failure case

Order 17 reached `ZOHO_SYNC_FAILED` because test data contained a free-text address. This is useful evidence of failure handling, but distinguish a genuine provider rejection from malformed or unrealistic test input. Preserve the recovery test without presenting it as a normal customer-order scenario.
